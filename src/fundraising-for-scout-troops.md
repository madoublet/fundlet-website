**Short answer:** A scout troop or pack can run a product fundraiser by selling a local shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the troop's name. Each scout gets a personal link, supporters order online, and sales are credited to that scout automatically — including into individual scout accounts, with no order form and no cash handled by a child.

Scouting invented the product drive, and the mechanics have barely changed in decades: a form, a neighborhood, a cash box, and a committee chair who spends a Saturday sorting cases by scout. The selling part works. Everything after it is the problem.

---

## Individual scout accounts, kept straight

Troops commonly credit a share of each scout's sales to that scout's own account for camp, gear, and high-adventure trips. Per-participant totals make that automatic and transparent — every family can see what their scout sold and what was credited, which is where most account disputes come from.

- A link per scout, shared by their family
- Running totals per scout all drive long
- Group by den, patrol, or rank
- Exportable records for the troop treasurer

---

## No door-to-door, no cash box

A scout can participate fully from home. Supporters order online and pay the shop, so no scout carries cash, no scout knocks on a stranger's door, and the troop's fundraising no longer depends on which neighborhoods a family can safely canvass. The committee chair stops being a bank.

---

## No sorting and distribution weekend

Because supporters order from the shop directly, the shop fulfills each order. There is no bulk delivery to a volunteer's garage, no sorting by scout, and no pickup night where three families forget to come.

---

## Grandparents, and the wider family

The traditional drive is limited to the blocks around a scout's house. A link reaches grandparents, aunts, uncles, and family friends anywhere in the country — which is usually where a scout's most willing supporters already are.

---

## Alongside your council's official sales

Most troops run a council-run popcorn or camp card sale on a fixed calendar. A troop-level product drive with a local shop fills the rest of the year — a spring campaign for summer camp, for instance — without conflicting with the council program or its timing.

---

## Fits how a troop is actually run

Troop leadership is volunteer and rotates. Setup is one onboarding form, tracking is automatic, and last year's records do not disappear when a committee chair ages out with their child.
