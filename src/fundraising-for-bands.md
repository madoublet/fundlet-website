**Short answer:** A band or music program can fundraise by selling a local shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the program's name. Each student gets a personal link, supporters order online, and sales are credited to the student automatically — which scales to a 200-member marching band without anyone tallying order forms.

Band programs face a specific combination: a large roster, a trip deposit with a hard deadline, instrument and uniform costs that fall unevenly on families, and a director who is already teaching five classes and running rehearsals. The fundraiser has to run itself.

---

## Scales to a full marching band

A paper drive gets exponentially worse as the roster grows — 180 brochures out, 90 back, three weekends of counting. A link-based drive does not change difficulty with size. Upload the roster once and every student has a link; the tracking is identical whether the program has 20 students or 300.

- Bulk roster import rather than manual entry
- Group participants by section, grade, or ensemble
- Per-student totals for individual trip accounts
- One dashboard for the whole program

---

## Sections, ensembles, and feeder schools

Programs are rarely one group. Marching band, jazz band, color guard, percussion, and the middle-school feeder program can each be grouped within a campaign or run as campaigns of their own. Section leaders can see their own numbers, which turns the drive into something students help run rather than something done to them.

---

## Trip deposits and hard deadlines

Festival entries, bowl-game trips, and clinician fees have non-negotiable dates. A campaign with a fixed close lets you run a focused four-week drive timed to land before a deposit is due, and run a second one later in the year for the balance — instead of carrying a shortfall and asking families to cover it.

---

## Individual student accounts

Many programs credit a student's sales toward their own trip or instrument costs. Per-participant totals make that straightforward and transparent: each family can see exactly what their student sold and what it covered, which heads off the disputes that individual-account policies otherwise generate.

---

## Supporters beyond the student body

Band programs have an unusually large natural audience — alumni, parents of graduates, community members who watch halftime every Friday. Those people never see a brochure that goes home in a student's folder, but they will click a link shared by the program's social accounts and buy something real from a local shop.
