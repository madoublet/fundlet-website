**Short answer:** A school can run a product fundraiser through any local shop using Fundlet. The shop publishes a branded campaign page in the school's name, each student gets a personal link to share with family, and every order is credited to that student automatically. Nothing is collected in cash, nothing is tallied by hand, and the school is not locked into a fundraising company's catalog or margin.

The traditional school fundraiser is a logistics problem disguised as a sales problem. Brochures go home, most do not come back, the ones that do are filled in wrong, and a volunteer spends a weekend sorting envelopes while the office fields calls about missing orders. Every one of those steps exists only because the orders were on paper.

---

## No brochures, no envelopes, no tallying

Fundlet replaces the entire paper chain with one link per student. Families order online and pay the shop directly, so the school never handles money, never chases a missing envelope, and never adds up a column.

- Students share a link instead of carrying a form
- Families check out with a card, from anywhere
- Orders are credited to the student automatically
- Payouts are calculated when the campaign closes

---

## Keep more of what you raise

Traditional fundraising companies typically keep a large share of the sale and dictate the product. With Fundlet, the arrangement is between the school and a shop you choose — often a local business that already wants to support you — and the split is whatever the two of you agree to. The money also stays in the community rather than leaving with a national vendor.

---

## Works by class, grade, or homeroom

Larger schools rarely want one undifferentiated total. Participants can be grouped, so a drive can be tracked by homeroom, grade level, or team, which is what makes classroom competitions and grade-level goals possible without extra bookkeeping.

---

## Student privacy and safety

Nobody goes door to door with a form listing names and addresses, and no student carries cash. Supporters transact with the shop, not with the student, and the school is not storing payment information at all.

---

## For PTAs, classrooms, and clubs alike

The same program covers the whole building. A PTA can run the school-wide drive while the band, the robotics team, and the eighth-grade trip each run their own campaign with its own goal — all from the same shop, at the same time, without competing for the same volunteer hours.
