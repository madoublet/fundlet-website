**Short answer:** A fraternity chapter can run a philanthropy or chapter fundraiser by selling a shop's products through a Fundlet campaign in the chapter's name. Each brother shares a personal link, supporters order online, and sales are credited per brother automatically — giving the chapter a documented total and a per-member participation record without anyone collecting cash.

The usual chapter fundraiser is an event: a tournament, a cookout, a car wash. Events are weather-dependent, require a permit and a weekend, and raise whatever happens to show up. They also produce almost no record of who actually participated.

---

## Not dependent on turnout

A link-based drive decouples fundraising from attendance. Brothers share links with family, hometown networks, and alumni, and supporters buy from anywhere. A rained-out Saturday no longer sets the semester's philanthropy total.

- No permit, venue, or event insurance
- Runs during a full semester or a single week
- Reaches supporters who would never attend an event
- Nothing to set up or clean up

---

## Per-brother participation records

Chapters are judged by nationals and by the IFC on philanthropy participation, and most have only a sign-in sheet to show for it. Per-participant totals replace that with real data: who shared, who sold, and how much — exportable when the standards report is due.

---

## No one is holding the chapter's money

A brother collecting cash or running payments through a personal account is a liability for him and for the chapter. Removing custody of funds entirely — supporters pay the shop's checkout — removes that risk along with the reimbursement cycle that follows it.

---

## Alumni, and the long tail

A chapter's alumni network is its largest untapped asset. Alumni respond to a specific, concrete ask far better than to a general appeal, and a product from a local shop near campus carries a connection that a donation link does not. One email with a campaign link reaches decades of brothers at once.

---

## Philanthropy and chapter operations, kept separate

Money raised for a designated philanthropy should never be mixed with money raised for chapter expenses. Run them as two campaigns with two goals and two totals, and the distinction is documented by default rather than argued about at a chapter meeting.
