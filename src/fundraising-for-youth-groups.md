**Short answer:** A youth group can fundraise by selling a local shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the group's name. Each student gets a personal link to share, supporters order online, and sales are credited to that student automatically — so earnings can be applied to each student's own camp or trip cost without anyone handling money.

Youth ministry fundraising is mostly about cost per student. Camp, a retreat weekend, or a summer trip has a per-head price, and the families who most need help are the least likely to ask. A drive that tracks individual earnings is the practical way to close that gap.

---

## Earnings that follow the student

When a student's sales are credited to their own trip cost, fundraising stops being a group obligation and becomes something a motivated student can use to pay their own way. Per-participant totals make that visible and fair: each family sees exactly what their student raised.

- A link per student, shared by text or social
- Live per-student totals toward their trip cost
- Group students by trip, grade, or small group
- Scholarship decisions based on real numbers

---

## Students can actually run it

Teenagers are good at sharing links and bad at keeping order forms intact. This drive plays to that: a student's entire job is to send a link and talk about the trip. No forms to lose, no money to carry, no deadline to collect by.

---

## Safe by design

No student goes door to door, no student holds cash, and no student's phone number or address is written on a form that circulates. Supporters transact with the shop, which also means the ministry is not storing any payment information.

---

## Parents who are tired of being asked

A youth ministry asks the same families repeatedly across a year. Selling a real product from a local shop changes the nature of the ask — a grandparent buying coffee or cookies does not experience it as a donation request, which is why these drives can be repeated without fatigue.

---

## Camp deposits and the summer calendar

Camp registration closes in spring and the deposit is due before that. Running a short campaign in February or March, timed to close before the deposit date, means the money arrives when the invoice does rather than after the ministry has already fronted it.
