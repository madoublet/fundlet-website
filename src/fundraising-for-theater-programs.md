**Short answer:** A theater or drama program can fundraise by selling a shop's products through a Fundlet campaign in the program's name. Cast and crew each get a personal link, supporters order online, and every sale is credited automatically — so the drive does not need rehearsal time, a box of order forms, or a parent volunteer counting cash during tech week.

Theater budgets are front-loaded in a way that makes them awkward to fund. Performance rights, scripts, and royalties must be paid before a single ticket is sold, set materials and costumes follow immediately, and ticket revenue only arrives at the very end — if the house fills.

---

## Money before the curtain, not after

Ticket sales are the wrong instrument for covering pre-production costs, because they settle months too late. A campaign scheduled to close before rights are licensed turns that around: the program raises against a known expense ahead of time rather than borrowing against hoped-for attendance.

---

## Cast and crew both count

Crew, pit, and tech students are routinely left out of fundraising recognition because paper drives track only whoever brought back a form. With a participant list, everyone involved in the production has a link and a total — which matters for programs that credit sales toward festival trips or thespian society dues.

- Per-participant totals for cast, crew, and pit
- Group participants by production or by role
- Rehearsal time stays rehearsal time
- Records export for the booster or parent group

---

## Runs on the production calendar

A drive that needs weekly collection meetings cannot coexist with tech week. This one needs no meetings at all: students share links, supporters check out, and the dashboard reports itself. You can open a campaign at auditions and close it before opening night without touching it in between.

---

## Festivals, conferences, and competition travel

State festivals and thespian conferences arrive with registration fees, hotel blocks, and a bus. Because sales can be tracked per student, programs can apply a student's earnings to their own travel cost, which is usually the difference between a delegation of six and a delegation of twenty.

---

## An audience that already exists

Theater programs have a mailing list, a social following, and an alumni network — people who buy tickets every year and will happily buy a local shop's products to support a show. A shareable link reaches all of them, including the ones who have moved away.
