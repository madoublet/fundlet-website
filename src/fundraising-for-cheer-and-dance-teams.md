**Short answer:** A cheer or dance team can fundraise by selling a local shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the team's name. Each athlete shares a personal link, supporters order online, and every sale is credited to the right athlete automatically — which is what makes it practical to apply earnings to individual competition and travel costs.

Competitive cheer and dance carry some of the highest per-athlete costs in youth sports: choreography fees, music, two or three uniforms, camp, competition entries, and a nationals trip that has to be booked months out. Families feel those costs individually, so the fundraiser has to work individually too.

---

## Individual accounts, honestly tracked

The defining feature of a cheer or dance fundraiser is that earnings often follow the athlete. Fundlet gives every participant a running total, so a team can credit sales toward that athlete's own competition fees and show each family precisely where their number came from.

- Per-athlete totals, visible throughout the campaign
- Group participants by squad, level, or age division
- Exportable records for the team treasurer
- Clear basis for individual-account policies

---

## Deadlines that do not move

Choreography is due before the season. Nationals deposits are due before you know who qualifies. A campaign with a fixed close date lets you run a three-week drive that lands money before the deadline, rather than a year-long effort that arrives after you have already fronted the cost.

---

## Squads and levels in one program

A gym or program with mini, youth, junior, and senior squads can run them as separate campaigns or as one campaign with grouped participants. Either way each squad sees its own goal, and the program sees the whole picture — useful when a single fundraiser has to cover shared expenses like mats, music, and coaching travel.

---

## Products families are glad to buy

Cheer and dance families are asked for money constantly, which is why a product drive outperforms another donation request. Supporters get something real — baked goods, candles, coffee, treats — from a local shop, at a normal price, for a cause they are already connected to. The ask is easier to make and easier to repeat next season.

---

## Travel without the shoebox

Nationals travel means collecting money from twenty families on different schedules. Online checkout removes the shoebox of checks and cash entirely: supporters pay the shop, the shop pays out on the campaign, and the team's records are a report rather than a stack of envelopes.
