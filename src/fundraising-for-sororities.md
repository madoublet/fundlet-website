**Short answer:** A sorority chapter can run a philanthropy or chapter fundraiser by selling a shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the chapter's name. Each sister gets a personal link, supporters order online, and every sale is credited to the sister who shared the link — producing both the funds and the per-member participation record nationals asks for.

Chapter fundraising has an accountability problem more than a sales problem. Philanthropy requirements are measured per member, Venmo requests scatter across a hundred phones, and the philanthropy chair ends up reconstructing who did what from screenshots the week the report is due.

---

## Participation you can actually measure

Most chapters require each member to contribute to philanthropy, and most have no reliable way to record it. A per-participant total is a clean answer: every sister has a link, and her sales are hers on the dashboard, all semester.

- A link per member, shared however she likes
- Live per-member totals, no self-reporting
- Group by pledge class, family, or committee
- Exportable records for the chapter report

---

## No collecting money from your sisters

Cash and peer-to-peer payment apps are the worst part of chapter fundraising: the chair fronts money, chases reimbursements, and holds a balance she is personally responsible for. Here, supporters pay the shop at a normal online checkout. The chair never takes custody of a dollar.

---

## Alumnae are your best audience

A chapter's reach is far larger than its active roster. Alumnae are genuinely willing to support the chapter and are almost never asked in a way that is easy to say yes to. A link in an alumnae newsletter or Facebook group converts, and a shop can ship anywhere in the country.

---

## Philanthropy partners and national causes

Chapters raise for a designated national philanthropy, for local service partners, and sometimes for chapter operations. Running them as separate campaigns keeps the accounting honest — each cause has its own page, its own goal, and its own total, which matters when you are reporting to a national organization about restricted funds.

---

## Recruitment, Greek Week, and the calendar

The chapter calendar is crowded and the windows are short. A campaign timed to a philanthropy week, Greek Week, or a Parents Weekend can open and close in two weeks, run entirely on shared links, and require nothing of the chapter during formal recruitment.
