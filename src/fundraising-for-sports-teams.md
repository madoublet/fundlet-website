**Short answer:** A sports team can run a product fundraiser through any shop that uses Fundlet. The shop lists its products on a branded campaign page in your team's name, each player gets a personal link to share, and every order placed through that link is credited to the player automatically. There are no paper order forms, no cash to collect, and no tallying at the end — the payout is calculated for you.

Team fundraising usually fails for logistical reasons, not for lack of support. Families *will* buy, and relatives two states away *would* buy — but the order form never reaches them, the envelope of cash goes missing, and a coach or team parent loses a weekend adding up columns. Fundlet removes every one of those steps.

---

## Built around a roster

Most fundraising tools treat your team as a single bucket of donations. Fundlet treats it as a roster. Each player is a participant with their own link and their own running total, which is what you need if you want to recognize top sellers, set an individual minimum, or credit sales toward a player's own account for travel costs.

- Add your roster once, from a phone, at a practice
- Each player gets a link to text, post, or AirDrop
- Sales are attributed the moment a supporter checks out
- Totals by player are visible all campaign long

---

## Travel, tournaments, and off-season gaps

Sports costs arrive in lumps — a tournament entry in March, a uniform order in July, a bus deposit the week before a playoff run. Because a campaign has its own start and end dates, you can run a short, sharp drive ahead of each expense instead of one annual push that has to cover everything.

Running two drives in the same season is normal: a fall campaign for uniforms and a spring campaign for travel, each with its own goal and its own total, both from the same roster.

---

## Grandparents, alumni, and the out-of-town problem

The biggest limit on a traditional team fundraiser is geography. A player can only knock on doors they can reach. A link travels anywhere — which means grandparents, former teammates, and family friends in other states become real customers, and the shop ships to them like any other order.

---

## What your families see

Supporters see a normal online store with your team's name and goal on it. They pay the shop with a card at a standard checkout, get a confirmation email, and receive a real product. Nobody is asked to hand cash to a teenager, and no player carries an order form with other people's addresses and phone numbers on it.

---

## What it means for your treasurer

Every order is recorded, which makes reconciliation a matter of reading one report instead of reconstructing a season. You can export the records for your books, and because money moves through the shop's checkout rather than through players and parents, there is a clean paper trail for the whole drive.
