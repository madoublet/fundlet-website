**Short answer:** A robotics or STEM team can fundraise by selling a local shop's products through a Fundlet campaign page in the team's name. Students share personal links, supporters order online, and every sale is credited to a student and recorded — which gives the team both the money and the documentation it needs for sponsors and grant reports.

Competitive robotics is expensive in a way that surprises new teams. Registration for a single regional runs into the thousands, the parts budget is consumed and replaced every build season, and advancing to a championship means travel costs you find out about with weeks of notice.

---

## Build season has no spare hours

Between kickoff and competition there is no slack. A fundraiser that requires meetings, collection, and counting will be abandoned by week two. This one requires an afternoon of setup: enter the roster, share the links, and let the dashboard do the reporting while the team builds.

---

## Documentation sponsors actually ask for

Corporate sponsors and grant programs want to see that a team has a funding plan and can account for what it raises. Because every order is captured with its campaign and participant, the drive produces an exportable record you can attach to a sponsorship report — and a demonstrated community-fundraising track record, which strengthens the next ask.

- Exportable transaction records per campaign
- Goal tracking you can show a sponsor mid-season
- Per-student totals for outreach and service hours
- Multiple campaigns across a season, separately reported

---

## Outreach requirements, usefully met

Most competition programs score or require community outreach. A drive run with a local business is genuine outreach: students approach a shop, present the partnership, and operate the campaign. It is a better story in a judging interview than a bake sale, and the award criteria in programs like FIRST reward exactly this kind of sustained community engagement.

---

## When you advance unexpectedly

Qualifying for a championship is good news with a four-figure invoice attached and a short window to pay it. A short campaign can be opened and closed inside that window, because there is no brochure to print, no distribution, and no collection period — only links and online checkout.

---

## Parts, tools, and the recurring budget

Unlike a one-time trip, a parts budget recurs every season. Running the same program annually with the same shop makes the team's funding predictable, and returning supporters already know how it works.
