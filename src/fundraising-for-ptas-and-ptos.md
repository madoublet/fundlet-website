**Short answer:** A PTA or PTO can run a product fundraiser through a shop using Fundlet. The shop hosts a branded campaign page for your school, students share personal links, and families order online. Because every order is recorded and attributed automatically, the treasurer's reconciliation is a report rather than a weekend of counting envelopes.

PTA boards turn over every year or two, and almost all the work is unpaid. The fundraiser that survives that turnover is the one that does not depend on any single volunteer being unusually organized.

---

## Volunteer hours, honestly accounted for

The hidden cost of a paper drive is labor: distribution, collection, sorting, counting, depositing, chasing missing orders, and distributing product. Fundlet removes the collection and counting stages entirely, which is typically where the volunteer hours go.

- One onboarding form instead of a distribution plan
- No money counted, deposited, or reimbursed
- No order sorting — the shop fulfills each order directly
- Progress visible to the whole board, live

---

## Clean books for the treasurer

Every order is captured with its campaign and participant, and the records export. For an organization that files a treasurer's report at every meeting and often an annual return, that difference matters more than the convenience: the drive produces its own audit trail instead of requiring one to be reconstructed.

---

## Cash handling policies

Many districts and national PTA guidelines restrict how volunteers may handle cash, require dual counting, and discourage money moving through students. A drive where supporters pay a merchant's checkout directly sidesteps most of that policy surface, because the PTA never takes custody of the funds en route.

---

## Multiple goals in one year

Fall carnival, spring teacher grants, playground equipment, field-trip subsidies. Rather than one annual drive that has to fund all of it, you can run a short campaign per goal, each with its own page and target, so families see precisely what they are supporting each time.

---

## Equity across families

Paper drives quietly advantage students whose parents have the time and network to sell. A shareable link narrows that gap — out-of-town relatives can participate, and a student is not penalized for living somewhere with nobody to canvass. For families who would rather not sell at all, nothing obliges them to.
