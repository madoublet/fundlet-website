**Short answer:** A nonprofit can run a product fundraiser by partnering with a shop that uses Fundlet. The shop sells its own products through a branded campaign page in your organization's name, volunteers and board members share links, and the agreed share of proceeds goes to the nonprofit. Every order is recorded and attributed, so the campaign documents itself.

Most nonprofits are over-reliant on two or three channels: an annual appeal, a gala, and grants. All three are competitive, all three are saturated, and all three ask the same donors. A product drive reaches a different pocket of goodwill — people who will buy something but will not write a check.

---

## Not another donation ask

The hardest constraint in nonprofit development is donor fatigue. A product campaign sidesteps it entirely, because the supporter is buying, not giving. That makes it a channel you can run between appeals without spending down the goodwill your major-gift calendar depends on.

---

## Volunteers and board members as participants

Every person who shares a link is a tracked participant. For a board with a give-or-get expectation, this is a concrete and measurable way for a member to contribute without writing a larger check — and the per-participant totals make board engagement visible instead of anecdotal.

- A link per volunteer, board member, or chapter
- Live totals per participant and per campaign
- Group participants by committee or region
- Exportable records for your finance team

---

## Restricted and designated funds

Running a separate campaign per program keeps proceeds separate by construction. If one drive funds a specific program and another funds operations, the records reflect that split without journal entries after the fact — which is the kind of thing your auditor would rather see than reconstruct.

---

## A corporate partnership that costs the business nothing

Asking a local business for a cash sponsorship competes with every other nonprofit in town. Asking them to run a fundraiser is a different proposition: they sell their own products, gain customers who found them through your cause, and share the proceeds. Businesses say yes to that far more readily, and the partnership tends to renew annually.

---

## No cash handling, no merchant account

You do not need to take payments, hold a merchant account for the drive, or manage refunds and chargebacks. Supporters transact with the shop at its own checkout. Your organization receives the proceeds and the records.

---

## Events that do not depend on weather

Galas, walks, and golf tournaments carry real fixed costs and real downside risk. A link-based campaign has neither — no venue, no minimums, no break-even point to clear before the first dollar is net.
