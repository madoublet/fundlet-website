**Short answer:** A community club or association can run a product fundraiser by partnering with a shop that uses Fundlet. The shop sells its own products through a branded campaign page in the club's name, members share links, and the agreed share of proceeds funds the club's work. Orders are recorded and attributed per member, with no cash collected.

Service clubs, veterans' posts, alumni associations, garden clubs, historical societies, and neighborhood associations all share a shape: a small, committed membership, a shrinking pool of volunteer hours, and a budget funded almost entirely by their own effort.

---

## Scales down to a small membership

Most fundraising advice assumes a large organization. A club of thirty members cannot staff an event, but thirty members sharing links to their own networks reaches several thousand people — which is the one form of leverage a small, long-tenured membership genuinely has.

- A link per member, however they prefer to share
- Live per-member and per-campaign totals
- Group members by committee or chapter
- Exportable records for the treasurer's report

---

## Scholarships, grants, and the annual commitment

Many clubs commit to a recurring obligation: a scholarship, a grant to a local charity, a park bench, a holiday food drive. A campaign per commitment, scheduled ahead of when the money is due, replaces the scramble to make up a shortfall at the end of the year.

---

## Younger members, and a drive they recognize

Clubs worry, correctly, about recruiting members under fifty. A fundraiser that runs on shared links rather than on a Saturday barbecue is one a newer member can participate in around a job and small children — which makes membership feel possible rather than onerous.

---

## No event, no permit, no break-even

Pancake breakfasts, fish fries, and raffles come with permits, insurance, licensing rules that vary by state, and fixed costs to clear before anything is net. A product campaign has no venue, no minimum, and no downside if turnout would have been poor.

---

## Alumni and former members

Alumni associations and veterans' posts have enormous dormant networks — people who moved away, drifted off the active roster, and still care. An email with a campaign link reaches all of them at once, and a shop can ship anywhere in the country.

---

## Records for the treasurer

Every order is captured with its campaign and participant and exports cleanly. For an organization that reads a treasurer's report into the minutes at every meeting, the drive produces its own documentation rather than requiring someone to assemble it.
