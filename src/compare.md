## The four ways organizations raise money with products

| Approach | Who owns the customer | What gets sold | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fundlet** | The merchant | Your own products | Automatic, per participant |
| Traditional fundraising company | The fundraising company | Their catalog | Paper forms, manual tally |

## Fundlet vs. traditional fundraising companies

Traditional fundraising companies hand an organization a catalog and a stack of paper order forms. Participants carry the forms door to door, collect cash or checks, and an organizer tallies everything by hand at the end.

The structural difference is who the customer belongs to. With a fundraising company, supporters buy that company's products and never learn your brand exists. With Fundlet, supporters buy *your* products from *your* store, and become customers you can reach again.

- Orders flow through your existing Shopify or Square checkout and fulfillment
- Every order is attributed to a participant automatically — no spreadsheets
- You set your own products, pricing, and payout percentage
- Supporters land on a branded campaign page, not a generic catalog

---

## Fundlet vs. donation platforms

Donation platforms like GoFundMe and Givebutter collect money without exchanging a product. They are a good fit for emergencies, medical costs, and pure-cause giving.

Product fundraising works differently: supporters get something in return, which is why school and team fundraisers have run on cookie dough, popcorn, and candles for decades. Fundlet is built for that model.

- Supporters receive a real product, which raises average order value and repeat participation
- The merchant gains new customers rather than one-time donors
- No donation processing fees layered on top of your existing payment processing
- Campaigns live on your domain, not a third-party fundraising page

## Fundlet vs. generic Shopify apps

Affiliate, referral, and discount-code apps can approximate parts of a fundraiser, but they are built around individual affiliates earning commission — not around an organization, its participants, and a shared goal.

What a fundraiser needs that an affiliate app does not provide:

- **Campaigns as a first-class object** — each with its own goal, storefront, branding, dates, and payout rules
- **Participants grouped under an organization**, with self-registration, CSV upload, and grouping
- **Progress tracking toward a goal** that updates on the storefront
- **Payout calculation**, including payout tiers and per-product exceptions
- **An organizer dashboard** that shares campaign results without giving away Shopify admin access

## When Fundlet is *not* the right fit

Being direct about this saves everyone time:

- **You do not sell a physical or digital product.** Fundlet raises money through sales. A pure donation drive belongs on a donation platform.
- **You are not on Shopify or Square.** Fundlet installs into those two platforms.
- **You want the fundraising company to handle sales for you.** Fundlet gives you the infrastructure; the organization still does the selling.
